## Southwest Carry-on Policy

Southwest Airlines allows passengers to bring carry-on bags, but there are specific rules. Understanding these rules helps avoid extra fees or delays at the gate. The airline has clear limits on the size and weight of carry-on items. Passengers can bring one carry-on bag plus one personal item. Knowing what counts as a carry-on and personal item is important for smooth travel.

### Allowed Carry-on Items

Southwest permits one carry-on bag and one personal item per passenger. The carry-on bag fits in the overhead bin. The personal item fits under the seat in front. Common personal items include:

* Purse
* Laptop bag
* Small backpack
* Briefcase

Carry-on bags may include:

* Suitcase
* Duffel bag
* Backpack

Items like strollers or medical equipment can also be brought onboard but may have different rules.

### Size And Weight Limits

Southwest sets size limits to ensure bags fit in the cabin. Carry-on bags must not exceed 10 x 16 x 24 inches.

No specific weight limit applies to carry-on bags. Still, bags must be light enough to lift into the overhead bin. Personal items should be small and easy to store under the seat.

Following these size limits helps avoid having to check bags at the gate, which can cause delays and fees.

## Rules For Two Carry-on Bags

Southwest Airlines allows passengers to bring two bags on board. These include one carry-on bag and one personal item. Understanding the rules helps avoid extra fees and hassle at the gate. Each bag has size and weight limits to follow.

### What Counts As A Carry-on

A carry-on bag is any larger item you bring to the cabin. It fits in the overhead bin. Common examples are small suitcases and backpacks. Southwest's maximum carry-on size is 10 x 16 x 24 inches.

Bags larger than these dimensions must be checked. Carry-on bags should be light enough to lift overhead. Southwest does not weigh carry-ons but enforces size rules strictly.

### Personal Item Vs. Carry-on Bag

A personal item is smaller and fits under the seat. Examples include purses, laptops, or small backpacks. This item must be smaller than the carry-on bag.

Southwest allows one personal item and one carry-on per passenger. Both must fit within their size limits. Personal items do not count towards carry-on size limits but cannot be bulky.

Keep the personal item easy to access. It holds essentials like documents, snacks, or electronics. Knowing these rules helps avoid confusion at boarding.

### Overhead Bin Space

Southwest allows you to bring one carry-on bag and one personal item free of charge. But what if you have two carry-ons? This often means one bag might need to be gate-checked if overhead bin space is tight.

Overhead bins fill up quickly, especially on busy flights. Arriving early and boarding sooner can increase your chances of finding space for both bags. Think about how you pack—lighter, more compact bags make fitting easier for everyone.

Have you noticed how some travelers manage to stash bulky items under the seat? Your personal item should fit there comfortably, freeing up bin space for your carry-on.

### Gate-checking Bags

When overhead bins are full, Southwest may ask you to gate-check one of your carry-on bags. This means your bag travels in the cargo hold but is returned to you at the gate after landing.

Gate-checking is quick and convenient if you’re prepared. Use a brightly colored tag or ribbon to easily spot your bag when you collect it. Also, pack essential items in your personal item to avoid inconvenience while waiting.

Have you ever had to gate-check a bag unexpectedly? Being ready with this option can save you stress and keep your trip on track.
