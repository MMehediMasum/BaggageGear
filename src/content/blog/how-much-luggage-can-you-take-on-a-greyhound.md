## Greyhound Luggage Allowance

Understanding the Greyhound luggage allowance is essential before you pack for your trip. Knowing exactly how much and what type of baggage you can bring helps avoid surprises and extra fees at the station. It also ensures a smoother, stress-free travel experience.

### Carry-on Bag Limits

Greyhound allows you to bring one carry-on bag on board at no extra cost. This bag must fit under the seat or in the overhead rack. The maximum size allowed is typically 14 inches by 22 inches by 9 inches.

Think of your carry-on as your personal space for essentials. It’s a good idea to pack items you might need during the trip, like snacks, a book, or electronics. Overpacking your carry-on can lead to delays or having to check the bag instead.

### Checked Bag Rules

For checked luggage, Greyhound lets you check up to two bags per ticket, with each bag weighing no more than 50 pounds. The size limit for each checked bag is 62 linear inches (length + width + height).

If your bags exceed the weight or size limits, expect additional fees. These fees vary depending on the route and time of travel. You can save money by weighing and measuring your bags before you arrive at the station.

### Additional Item Policies

Besides your carry-on and checked bags, Greyhound allows a few additional items without extra charge. These include one small personal item like a purse, briefcase, or laptop bag.

Special equipment such as strollers, wheelchairs, or assistive devices are also accepted but should be declared in advance. If you’re carrying sports gear or musical instruments, check Greyhound’s specific rules beforehand—they might need to be tagged or stored differently.

### Sports Equipment

Greyhound accepts some sports gear with certain limits. Large items like bicycles must be boxed or bagged. Golf clubs and skis need to fit within size limits. Fragile items should be packed carefully to avoid damage. Some equipment might require extra fees or advance notice.

### Musical Instruments

Small instruments can travel as carry-on bags. Larger instruments need to be checked as luggage. Protect your instrument with a sturdy case. Early arrival helps secure space for your instrument. Avoid placing instruments with fragile or sharp parts in overhead bins.

### Pets And Service Animals

Only service animals are allowed inside the bus. Service dogs must stay with their owner at all times. Pets must travel in approved carriers in the luggage compartment. Carriers should be leak-proof and well-ventilated. Notify Greyhound in advance when traveling with animals.

If your luggage doesn’t arrive or is damaged, report it immediately at the Greyhound station. You need to fill out a Property Irregularity Report (PIR) before leaving the terminal. This report is your official record and key to any claim you might make later.

Make sure to keep copies of all paperwork and keep your ticket handy. Reporting delays or damages after leaving the station can complicate the process. Have you ever waited too long to report an issue and found it harder to get help?

### Compensation Guidelines

Greyhound offers compensation based on the value of the lost or damaged items, but there are limits. They typically reimburse up to a certain dollar amount, which might not cover high-value items fully. It’s important to check their current policies before you travel.

Keep receipts or proof of purchase for your belongings. Without them, you might receive less compensation. Think about whether you need extra insurance for expensive gear—sometimes, it’s worth the peace of mind.

### Preventive Measures

Preventing luggage loss or damage starts with how you pack and label your bags. Use sturdy suitcases with secure locks and add clear identification tags with your contact information. Avoid packing valuable or fragile items in checked luggage.
