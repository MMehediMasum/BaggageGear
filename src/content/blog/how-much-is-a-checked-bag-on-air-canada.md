## Air Canada Baggage Policy

Understanding Air Canada’s baggage policy is essential before you pack your bags. Knowing the rules helps you avoid unexpected fees and ensures a smooth travel experience. Let’s break down what you need to know about carry-on and checked bags, including size and weight limits.

### Carry-on Vs Checked Bags

You can bring one carry-on bag and one personal item on board without extra charges. Carry-ons must fit in the overhead bin, while personal items should slide under the seat in front of you.

* **Carry-on bags:**Ideal for essentials and valuables you want close at hand.
* **Checked bags:**Useful for heavier or bulkier items that don’t need to be with you during the flight.

Deciding whether to carry on or check your bag depends on your trip length and what you’re bringing. Have you ever had to gate-check your carry-on because the cabin was full? That’s a good reason to think about checking a bag ahead of time.

### Size And Weight Limits

Air Canada sets clear size and weight limits to keep things fair and safe. Your carry-on bag should not exceed 23 cm x 40 cm x 55 cm (9 in x 15.5 in x 21.5 in), including handles and wheels.

Checked bags can be larger, but each must weigh no more than 23 kg (50 lbs) in Economy Class. If your bag is heavier or bigger, prepare for extra fees or consider splitting your items.

| Bag Type | Maximum Size | Maximum Weight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Carry-On | 23 x 40 x 55 cm (9 x 15.5 x 21.5 in) | Not specified |
| Checked Bag (Economy) | 158 cm total (length + width + height) | 23 kg (50 lbs) |

Have you measured your bag recently? Sometimes luggage grows with all those souvenirs you pick up. Checking size and weight before you leave saves time and stress at the airport.

## Standard Checked Bag Fees

Standard checked bag fees on Air Canada vary by route and fare type. Knowing these fees helps plan your trip budget better. Fees apply to most checked bags unless your fare includes free checked luggage. The size and weight limits for standard bags usually stay consistent across flights. Paying for bags online before your flight often costs less than at the airport.

### Domestic Flights

On domestic flights within Canada and the U.S., the first checked bag fee is generally around $30 to $35 CAD/USD. The second bag fee is higher, usually about $50 to $60 CAD/USD. Bags must not exceed 23 kg (50 lbs) and 158 cm (62 inches) in total size. Overweight or oversized bags incur extra charges. Fees may vary by specific route or promotions.

### International Flights

International checked bag fees depend on your destination. Flights to Europe or Asia often include one free checked bag in economy class. Flights to the U.S. and Mexico may charge for the first bag, around $30 USD. Fees for additional bags increase with each piece. Weight limits are similar, but some regions allow up to 32 kg (70 lbs) per bag without fees.

### Basic Economy Vs Other Classes

Basic Economy passengers usually pay for all checked bags. No free checked bag allowance comes with this fare. Standard Economy often includes one free checked bag on many international routes. Premium Economy, Business, and Signature Class fares include two or more free checked bags. Paying extra for upgraded classes can save money on baggage fees for longer trips.

### Second And Third Checked Bags

Checking one bag is usually straightforward, but what happens if you need to bring more? Air Canada charges extra fees for second and third checked bags, and these fees vary depending on your route.

* On domestic flights within Canada and to/from the U.S., the second checked bag typically costs around $50 to $70.
* The third checked bag can cost even more, often between $100 and $150.
* International flights have different rates, so it’s a good idea to check the specific fees for your destination on Air Canada’s website before packing.

I once traveled to Europe with extra gear and didn’t check these fees in advance. The surprise charges at the counter were quite a hit to my budget. If you expect to carry multiple bags, consider pre-paying or adjusting your packing to avoid these extra costs.

### Overweight And Oversized Fees

Air Canada has strict limits on bag size and weight. Bags over 23 kg (50 lbs) usually face additional overweight fees, and those over 32 kg (70 lbs) may not be accepted at all.

| Weight Range | Fee (Approximate) |

| 23 kg to 32 kg (50-70 lbs) | $100 - $200 |
| Over 32 kg (70 lbs) | Not accepted or special arrangements needed |

Oversized bags, exceeding 158 cm (62 inches) in combined length, width, and height, also come with fees. These can add $100 or more to your bill. Have you ever had to pay extra because your bag was just a bit too heavy or large? Planning your packing and weighing your bags at home can help you avoid these costly surprises.

### Sports Equipment

Sports equipment like skis, snowboards, and golf clubs have unique rules. Air Canada allows sports gear as checked baggage but charges may apply. Fees depend on the item size and weight. Packing sports equipment properly is important to avoid damage. Some items require advance notice or special handling.

### Musical Instruments

Musical instruments can be checked or carried on board. Small instruments fit in overhead bins without extra cost. Larger instruments may need to be checked and may incur fees. Air Canada advises using a hard case for protection. Declaring your instrument at check-in helps ensure safe handling.

### Medical And Mobility Devices

Medical and mobility devices travel free of charge. This includes wheelchairs, walkers, and oxygen tanks. These items get priority handling and do not count toward baggage allowance. Passengers should inform Air Canada ahead to arrange special support. Proper packing and labeling help with smooth boarding.
