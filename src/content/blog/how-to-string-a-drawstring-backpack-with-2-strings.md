**Have you ever struggled with a drawstring backpack that just won't cooperate? You know the frustration: one string is longer than the other, or they’re tangled beyond belief.**

But what if you could fix this in just a few simple steps? Imagine the ease of having a perfectly strung backpack that opens and closes smoothly every time. In this guide, you'll learn how to string a drawstring backpack with two strings like a pro.

Say goodbye to lopsided straps and hello to convenience and style. Read on, and you'll never have to wrestle with your backpack strings again.




## Materials Needed

Before you start stringing your drawstring backpack, gathering the right materials is crucial. The quality and type of strings you choose will affect both the look and durability of your backpack. Having the necessary tools ready makes the process smoother and less frustrating.

### Choosing The Right Strings

Not all strings are created equal for drawstring backpacks. You want something strong but flexible, like nylon or polyester cords, which resist fraying and stretching over time.

Consider the thickness of the strings too. Too thin, and they might dig into your shoulders; too thick, and they could be hard to thread through the holes. A diameter of around 5mm usually works well.

Color is another factor—do you want your strings to match the backpack or stand out as a style statement? Think about your daily use and how the strings will hold up against dirt and wear.

### Additional Tools

Besides the strings, a few simple tools can make your job easier. A safety pin or a small bodkin is great for threading the strings through the backpack’s holes without hassle.

You might also need scissors to cut the strings to the right length and a lighter or tape to seal the ends and prevent fraying. Having these tools on hand saves time and avoids last-minute trips to the store.

Do you have these materials ready, or is there something you haven’t thought about yet? Preparing well can make your project enjoyable and give your backpack a professional finish.

## Preparing The Backpack

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=preparing-the-backpack-is-an-important-first-step-before-threading-the-drawstrings.-it-makes-the-process-easier-and-faster.&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Preparing the backpack is an important first step before threading the drawstrings. It makes the process easier and faster. A clean, flat backpack helps you see the parts clearly. This preparation also prevents tangling and damage during stringing.

### Emptying And Flattening

Remove all items from inside the backpack. Check every pocket and compartment. A completely empty backpack lays flat and smooth. Spread the bag on a clean surface. Press it down gently to remove wrinkles and folds. This gives clear access to the openings and eyelets.

### Locating The Eyelets

Eyelets are small metal or plastic rings where the strings pass through. Find these holes on each side of the backpack. They usually sit near the bottom corners or along the sides. Check both inside and outside for hidden eyelets. Clear any fabric or dirt covering them. Knowing their exact positions helps guide the strings correctly.

## Starting The Stringing Process

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=starting-the-stringing-process-sets-the-base-for-a-secure-drawstring-backpack.-this-stage-requires-patience-and-a-clear-approach.&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Starting the stringing process sets the base for a secure drawstring backpack. This stage requires patience and a clear approach. It ensures the strings fit well and work properly.

The first task is to thread the strings through the backpack. Attention to detail here prevents tangles and uneven tension later.

### Inserting The First String

Hold one end of the string firmly. Push it through one of the backpack’s eyelets. Guide the string along the channel inside the fabric. Pull it out from the opposite eyelet carefully. Repeat this for the second side to create a loop. Make sure the string moves smoothly without twisting.

### Securing The String Ends

Once the string is in place, secure its ends. Tie a strong knot at each end to stop slipping. Trim any extra string if needed. Avoid knots too close to the eyelets. This keeps the drawstring balanced and easy to pull.

## Threading The Second String

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=threading-the-second-string-is-an-important-step-in-assembling-your-drawstring-backpack.-this-string-balances-the-first-and-completes-the-closure-system.&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Threading the second string is an important step in assembling your drawstring backpack. This string balances the first and completes the closure system. It must follow the exact path to work smoothly and look neat. Take your time to avoid tangles or uneven loops.

### Following The Correct Path

Start by inserting the second string into the same opening where the first string began. Pass it through the opposite side of the backpack's channel. Make sure the string runs along the inside edge of the fabric tube. This keeps the backpack's design tidy and functional.

Check that the string goes through the bottom corner eyelet. Then, pull it up through the other side’s channel. Repeat this on both sides until the string returns to the starting point. The string should mirror the path of the first string exactly.

### Adjusting The Length

After threading, pull both strings gently to even their lengths. Both should be the same size for proper closure. Adjust by sliding the string ends through the eyelets carefully. Ensure the strings are not twisted or tangled inside the channels.

Once equal, tie knots at the string ends to keep them secure. This prevents the strings from slipping back inside the backpack. Your drawstring backpack is now ready for use with a balanced and sturdy closure.

## Final Adjustments

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=after-threading-the-strings-through-your-drawstring-backpack,-making-final-adjustments-is-key-to-ensuring-comfort-and-functionality.-this-stage-is-where-your-backpack-truly-becomes-custom-fit-to-your-needs.&w=424&h=324&c=7)

After threading the strings through your drawstring backpack, making final adjustments is key to ensuring comfort and functionality. This stage is where your backpack truly becomes custom-fit to your needs. Small tweaks here can save you from frustration later on.

### Tying Knots

Securing the ends of your drawstrings with knots prevents them from slipping back through the eyelets. Use strong knots like the double overhand or figure-eight for extra hold.

* Leave a bit of extra string beyond the knot to allow future adjustments.
* Make sure the knots are tight but not so tight that you can’t untie them later.
* If you want a cleaner look, you can trim excess string after tying the knot, but avoid cutting too close.

Did you know a poorly tied knot can undo all your threading efforts? Take your time here to get it right.

### Testing The Drawstring

Once the knots are tied, put the backpack on and pull the strings to test the tension. It should feel snug but not uncomfortable on your shoulders.

* Adjust the length by loosening or tightening the knots if needed.
* Check if both strings pull evenly to avoid uneven weight distribution.
* Try moving around with the backpack to see if the strings stay in place.

Ask yourself: does the backpack feel secure? Is there any pinching or slipping? These simple checks help you avoid surprises during daily use.




## Troubleshooting Tips

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=stringing-a-drawstring-backpack-with-two-strings-can-sometimes-lead-to-common-hiccups.-these-issues-might-slow-you-down-or-cause-frustration-if-you-don’t-know-how-to-handle-them-quickly.&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Stringing a drawstring backpack with two strings can sometimes lead to common hiccups. These issues might slow you down or cause frustration if you don’t know how to handle them quickly. Let’s look at some practical troubleshooting tips that will help you fix those annoying problems and keep your project moving smoothly.

### Dealing With Tangled Strings

Tangled strings are one of the most frequent problems when working with drawstring backpacks. If you find your strings knotted or twisted, don’t pull hard—it usually makes things worse.

Instead, gently separate the strings by holding the knot and slowly teasing it apart with your fingers. You can use a straight pin or a needle to carefully loosen tight knots without damaging the strings.

Have you tried laying the backpack flat on a table? This helps keep the strings visible and makes untangling easier. Keep patience in mind; rushing often tightens knots even more.

### Fixing Uneven Lengths

Uneven strings can make your backpack uncomfortable and less functional. Before tying the final knots, always measure both strings to ensure they are the same length.

If you notice one string is longer, pull the strings through the loops to adjust their length. You can also mark the midpoint with a small piece of tape or a stitch to keep things balanced.

Wondering how to avoid this issue next time? Try threading the strings slowly and checking lengths frequently. This small step can save you from having to redo your work later.




## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Do You Start Stringing A Drawstring Backpack?

Begin by threading one string through the top left eyelet. Pull it across to the opposite bottom eyelet. Repeat with the second string starting from the top right eyelet. This creates the basic loop structure for your backpack strings.

### Can I Use Any Type Of String For My Backpack?

Yes, but choose durable and flexible strings like nylon or polyester. These materials resist wear and tear. Avoid fragile or slippery strings to ensure your backpack stays securely closed and comfortable to carry.

### How Do I Adjust The Length Of The Drawstrings?

Pull the strings evenly from both ends to tighten. To loosen, pull the backpack fabric while holding the strings. Tie knots near the ends if you need to shorten the strings for a better fit.

### What If One String Breaks During Use?

Replace the broken string immediately to maintain backpack function. Use the same threading method to insert a new string. Regularly check strings for wear to prevent sudden breaks.
