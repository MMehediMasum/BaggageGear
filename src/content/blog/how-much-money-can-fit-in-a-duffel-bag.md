U.S. bills, for instance, all share the same dimensions: 6.14 inches long and 2.61 inches wide. However, the thickness of a single bill is about 0.0043 inches. This means:

* A stack of 100 bills is roughly 0.43 inches thick.
* Multiplying that by the number of stacks you can fit gives a volume estimate.

Imagine you have a duffel bag with an internal volume of 3000 cubic inches. If you stack $100 bills tightly, you can calculate how many such stacks fit into that space. Have you ever tried stacking bills to see how thick a bundle gets? It’s a quick way to visualize cash volume.

### Impact Of Packaging On Capacity

How you package your cash greatly affects how much fits inside your bag. Loose bills take up more space due to air gaps, while tightly bundled stacks maximize capacity.

Using rubber bands or money straps can compress bills, reducing volume by up to 20%. On the other hand, wrapping bundles in plastic or placing them in bulky envelopes adds extra volume, reducing your capacity.

Think about your own experience packing items—loose versus organized packing makes a huge difference. You can apply the same principle to cash to get the most out of your duffel bag’s space.
