## Spirit Airlines Baggage Fees

Spirit Airlines is known for its ultra-low base fares, but baggage fees can quickly add up if you're not prepared. Understanding Spirit's baggage fee structure helps you avoid surprises at the airport. Let’s break down how much you might pay for carry-on bags, checked luggage, and any oversized or overweight items.

### Carry-on Bag Costs

Spirit allows you to bring a personal item, like a backpack or purse, for free, but carry-on bags come with a fee. Prices vary depending on when you pay:

* **Booking online in advance:**Usually between $35 and $45 per bag
* **At the airport:**Fees can jump to $65 or more

Paying for your carry-on bag early can save you a lot. Have you ever waited until check-in only to face a hefty fee? Booking ahead is a simple way to keep costs down and reduce stress.

### Checked Bag Charges

Checked bags are not included in the base fare either. Spirit charges per bag, and the price depends on when you add it to your reservation:

| When You Pay | Fee per Checked Bag |
| --- | --- |
| During booking | $30 - $40 |
| Before online check-in | $40 - $50 |
| At the airport | $55 or more |

Checking your bag early online is usually much cheaper. Do you really want to risk paying almost double by waiting until the airport? Planning ahead with your luggage can save you both money and hassle.

### Oversized And Overweight Fees

Spirit also charges extra if your bags are too big or heavy. Here’s what you need to know:

* **Oversized bags:**If your bag exceeds 62 linear inches (length + width + height), you could pay $100 or more
* **Overweight bags:**Bags weighing between 41 and 50 pounds usually cost an additional $30
* Bags over 50 pounds may not be accepted or will cost even more

I once packed a suitcase that was just a few pounds over the limit and ended up paying an extra $30 at the gate. It was a frustrating lesson that made me double-check my bags every time since. How much extra are you willing to pay to avoid repacking at the airport?

## Additional Baggage Policies

Spirit Airlines is known for its ultra-low fares, but understanding their additional baggage policies can save you from unexpected fees. Knowing what counts as a personal item versus a carry-on or special equipment helps you pack smarter and avoid surprises at the gate. Let’s break down the essentials so you can plan your trip without stress.

### Personal Item Guidelines

Your personal item is the one bag you can bring on board without extra charge. It must fit under the seat in front of you, which means size really matters. Spirit allows items like a small backpack, purse, or laptop bag.

Keep in mind the maximum dimensions are 18 x 14 x 8 inches. If your bag is just a bit too big, you might face a fee, even if it looks small. I once tried squeezing a slightly oversized tote under the seat and ended up paying $65 at the gate—definitely something to avoid.

### Special Items And Sports Equipment

Spirit treats special items and sports gear differently from regular bags. Items such as strollers, car seats, or musical instruments require specific handling. Sports equipment like bikes, skis, or surfboards often have their own fees and packaging rules.

Always check Spirit’s guidelines before you pack these items. For example, your bike may need to be in a hard case or bag, and fees vary based on the item’s size and weight. Have you ever had to pay extra for your gear? Planning ahead can help you save money and hassle.

### Carry-on Bag Size Limits

Spirit charges for carry-on bags that don’t fit the personal item category. Your carry-on must fit in the overhead bin, but there’s a catch: Spirit’s size limits are stricter than many other airlines.

The maximum carry-on dimensions allowed are 22 x 18 x 10 inches. If your bag is larger, you’ll be charged. I learned this the hard way when my suitcase was just an inch too tall, and I had to pay $55 at check-in. Measuring your bag before you head to the airport can prevent this.
