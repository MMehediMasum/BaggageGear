## What Is Bag Balm

Bag Balm is a soft ointment known for its soothing properties. Originally made for farmers, it has gained attention beyond its first purpose. This balm is often considered for skin care, including conditions like eczema. Understanding its background and components helps clarify its potential benefits.

### Origins And Ingredients

Bag Balm was created in the early 1900s in Vermont, USA. It was designed to protect cows’ udders from irritation and dryness. The formula has stayed mostly the same over time.

* Petrolatum: A moisturizing base that locks in moisture.
* Lanolin: Extracted from sheep wool, helps soften skin.
* Mineral oil: Adds extra smoothness and hydration.
* Phenol: A mild antiseptic for minor skin issues.

### Common Uses

Farmers first used Bag Balm to soothe cracked and dry skin on animals. Later, people found it helpful for their own skin problems. It is popular for:

* Dry, cracked hands and feet
* Minor cuts and scrapes
* Chapped lips
* Rough patches of skin
* Soothing irritated or inflamed areas

## Risks And Precautions

Using Bag Balm for eczema relief has some risks and precautions to consider. Knowing these helps protect sensitive skin and avoid irritation. Always be careful when applying new products on eczema-prone areas.

### Potential Allergens

Bag Balm contains lanolin, a common allergen for some people. Lanolin comes from wool and can cause redness or itching. The ointment also has petroleum jelly, which may clog pores for some users. Fragrances or preservatives might trigger skin reactions too. Testing on a small skin patch first reduces allergy risks.

### When To Avoid Bag Balm

Avoid Bag Balm on broken or infected skin. Applying it on open wounds may worsen infection. People with known lanolin allergies should skip this product. If skin becomes red, itchy, or swollen after use, stop immediately. Consult a doctor before using Bag Balm on children or sensitive skin.
