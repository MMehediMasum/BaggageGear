## Pressure Changes In Airplanes

Pressure changes inside an airplane can affect many items in your luggage. Soda cans, for example, are sealed and contain gas under pressure. Understanding how airplane pressure works helps explain if these cans might explode during flight.

### How Cabin Pressure Works

Airplanes fly at very high altitudes where the air pressure is low. To keep passengers safe and comfortable, cabins are pressurized. The cabin pressure is set to match conditions found at about 6,000 to 8,000 feet above sea level.

This pressure is lower than on the ground, but it stays steady inside the cabin. The pressurization system pumps air in and out to keep the pressure stable. This means the air pressure inside the cabin does not change quickly or drastically during flight.

### Impact On Containers

Soda cans are designed to hold carbonated drinks under pressure. The gas inside the can pushes outward on the container walls. At higher altitudes, the outside air pressure drops.

Because the pressure inside the can stays the same, the difference between inside and outside pressure increases. This can cause cans to bulge or even leak. However, the steady cabin pressure usually prevents cans from exploding.

In the cargo hold, pressure is also controlled but may vary slightly more than in the cabin. Still, modern aircraft keep pressure at safe levels to protect luggage and its contents.




## Soda Cans And Pressure Sensitivity

Soda cans are more sensitive to pressure changes than many travelers realize. This sensitivity can lead to unexpected issues when these cans are placed in checked luggage. Understanding what happens inside a soda can under varying pressure can help you decide whether it’s safe to pack them for a flight.

### Carbonation And Internal Pressure

Soda is carbonated, meaning it contains dissolved carbon dioxide gas. This gas creates pressure inside the can, keeping the soda fizzy and fresh. The pressure inside a typical soda can ranges from 30 to 50 psi, which is higher than atmospheric pressure.

When you fly, the air pressure in the cargo hold drops significantly. This drop can cause the gas inside the can to expand. Have you ever noticed how a soda can feels tighter or softer after a flight? That’s the internal pressure reacting to external changes.

If the pressure difference becomes too large, it can cause the can to bulge or even pop open. This is why many travelers worry about soda cans exploding during flights.

### Can Design And Durability

Soda cans are made from aluminum, designed to withstand a fair amount of pressure. The curved shape helps distribute pressure evenly, making the can surprisingly strong. However, they’re not invincible.

Small dents or damage to the can can weaken its structure. I once packed a few soda cans in my checked bag without cushioning, and one dented can leaked slightly after landing. That experience taught me to pack cans carefully if I must bring them.

Airlines also pressurize cargo holds to some extent, but not to cabin levels. This partial pressurization usually prevents cans from exploding. Still, the risk increases if the can is already compromised or if it’s exposed to temperature changes that raise internal pressure.

## Risks Of Packing Soda In Checked Bags

Packing soda cans in checked luggage carries certain risks. These risks stem from changes in pressure and temperature during flights. Soda cans are sealed containers filled with carbonated liquid. This creates pressure inside the can that can change under flight conditions. Understanding these risks helps travelers avoid damage and inconvenience.

### Potential For Explosion

Soda cans can explode due to pressure changes in the airplane's cargo hold. The cabin pressure is lower than at ground level. This causes the gas inside the can to expand. If the pressure becomes too high, the can may burst. Temperature fluctuations can also weaken the can’s structure. A burst can creates a mess and may damage other items.

### Consequences For Luggage And Belongings

An exploded soda can can ruin clothes and electronics inside the luggage. Sticky soda stains are hard to clean and may cause permanent damage. The strong smell of soda can also linger for days. Other passengers’ bags nearby may also be affected. Airlines may refuse liability for damage caused by liquid spills. This makes it risky to pack soda cans in checked bags.

Airlines have specific rules about carrying soda on planes, especially in checked and carry-on luggage. These rules exist not just for safety, but also to make sure your travel experience is smooth. Knowing these policies can save you from surprise fees or having your soda confiscated at security.

### Checked Vs Carry-on Restrictions

In checked luggage, soda cans are generally allowed but come with risks. Changes in air pressure can cause cans to expand or even burst, damaging your belongings. Airlines don’t usually ban soda in checked bags, but they warn about potential leaks.

Carry-on luggage has stricter rules. Liquids, including soda, must be in containers of 100 ml (3.4 oz) or less and fit into a clear, quart-sized bag. This means a full can of soda is not allowed in carry-ons through security checkpoints.

## Tips For Traveling With Soda

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=carrying-soda-on-a-trip-can-be-tricky.-pressure-changes-in-planes-may-cause-soda-cans-to-burst.&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Carrying soda on a trip can be tricky. Pressure changes in planes may cause soda cans to burst. Knowing how to pack soda safely helps avoid messes and wasted drinks. Simple steps protect your luggage and keep soda intact.

### Safe Packing Methods

Use strong plastic bags to contain any leaks. Wrap each soda can in bubble wrap or clothes for cushioning. Place cans upright in the middle of your suitcase. Avoid overpacking to reduce pressure on cans. Check the suitcase weight so it is not too heavy.

Consider sealing cans with tape around the opening. This adds extra protection against leaks. Keep soda away from sharp objects in the luggage. Store cans in a hard-sided suitcase for better protection.

### Alternatives To Carrying Soda

Buy soda after passing airport security. This prevents pressure changes from affecting cans. Carry powder drink mixes to add water later. Use reusable bottles with soda purchased at your destination.

Try soda tablets that dissolve in water. They take less space and avoid spills. Choose drinks in plastic bottles instead of cans. Plastic is less likely to burst under pressure changes.




## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can Soda Cans Explode In Checked Luggage On Planes?

Yes, soda cans can explode due to pressure changes in the cargo hold. Temperature and altitude shifts increase gas pressure inside cans, causing leaks or bursts. It's safer to avoid packing carbonated drinks in checked bags to prevent mess and damage.

### Why Do Soda Cans Burst During Flights?

Soda cans burst because cabin pressure drops at high altitudes. This lower pressure causes the gas inside the cans to expand, increasing internal pressure. The cans may not withstand this stress and can rupture or leak during the flight.

### Is It Safe To Pack Soda Cans In Checked Baggage?

It's generally unsafe to pack soda cans in checked bags. Pressure changes and temperature fluctuations may cause cans to leak or explode. Carrying them in carry-on luggage is safer, but always check airline rules regarding liquids and carbonated beverages.

### How Can I Prevent Soda Cans From Exploding In Luggage?

To prevent explosions, avoid packing full soda cans in checked luggage. If necessary, use sealed containers designed for pressure changes or drink the soda before packing. Keeping cans cool can also reduce internal pressure and risk of bursting.
