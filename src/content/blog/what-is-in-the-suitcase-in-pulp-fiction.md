The suitcase in *Pulp Fiction*is one of the film’s most famous mysteries. It appears several times, shining and glowing whenever it opens. The suitcase’s contents are never shown or explained. The suitcase drives key parts of the plot. It connects different characters and stories. Vincent and Jules are sent to retrieve it. This idea is one of the most talked-about. Some believe the suitcase contains the soul of Marcellus Wallace, the crime boss.

Fans point to the bandage on the back of Wallace’s neck as evidence. The glow represents the soul’s supernatural essence. It adds a spiritual layer to the story, making you wonder about the true cost of power.

Others suggest the glow is simply a cinematic device. It’s used to create suspense and curiosity without revealing anything specific.

Tarantino has been famously tight-lipped about what’s inside the suitcase. He once mentioned that the suitcase is a MacGuffin—a plot device that drives the story but whose specific nature doesn’t matter. This choice invites viewers to focus more on the characters and their reactions than on the object itself.

In interviews, Tarantino hinted that the glowing effect when the suitcase opens was inspired by classic cinema techniques. He wanted the suitcase to symbolize something valuable and mysterious without defining it explicitly. This approach encourages you to use your imagination and engage with the story on a deeper level.

### Creative Choices Behind The Mystery

The glowing suitcase allows the audience to project their own ideas onto it, making the film more interactive. Tarantino’s decision not to reveal the contents turns the suitcase into a symbol rather than a literal item. This creative choice ensures the focus remains on character dynamics and plot tension.
