## Suitcase Size Standards

Suitcase size standards help travelers avoid extra fees and packing problems. Airlines often set limits on suitcase dimensions. Knowing these rules helps choose the right bag for your trip. Suitcase size is usually measured in linear inches.

### What Are Linear Inches

Linear inches are the total of a suitcase's length, width, and height. Add these three measurements together to get the linear inches. For example, a suitcase 28 inches tall, 18 inches wide, and 16 inches deep has 62 linear inches (28 + 18 + 16 = 62). Airlines use linear inches to decide if your bag fits their size rules. This measurement includes wheels and handles.

### Common Suitcase Dimensions

Suitcases come in many sizes. Here are common dimensions and their typical linear inches:

* Small carry-on: 22 x 14 x 9 inches = 45 linear inches
* Medium checked bag: 26 x 18 x 12 inches = 56 linear inches
* Large checked bag: 28 x 20 x 14 inches = 62 linear inches

A 28-inch suitcase often reaches the 62 linear inches mark. This size usually meets most airlines’ checked bag size limits. Always check airline rules before packing to avoid surprises.

### Length, Width, And Height Components

A suitcase’s size includes three parts:

* **Length:**The longest side, usually from top to bottom.
* **Width:**The side across the suitcase, from left to right.
* **Height:**The depth, from front to back, including wheels and handles.

Each part counts toward the total size limit for airlines.

### How To Calculate Linear Inches

Linear inches mean adding length, width, and height together. Use a tape measure and follow these steps:

* Measure the length in inches.
* Measure the width in inches.
* Measure the height in inches, including wheels and handles.
* Add all three numbers to get the total linear inches.

For example, if length is 28 inches, width is 18 inches, and height is 16 inches, total linear inches equal 62 inches.

## Airline Baggage Policies

Understanding airline baggage policies is crucial before packing your 28 inch suitcase. Each airline sets specific rules about size and weight, which can directly affect your travel experience. Knowing these limits helps you avoid extra fees and last-minute repacking at the airport.

### Linear Inch Limits For Checked Bags

Airlines usually measure checked bags by linear inches—this is the sum of the length, width, and height of your suitcase. Most major airlines set the maximum allowed size at 62 linear inches for checked luggage. A typical 28 inch suitcase often hits this 62-inch mark, but it depends on the exact dimensions.

Check your suitcase measurements carefully. If your bag exceeds 62 linear inches, the airline may charge an oversized baggage fee. This fee can be quite expensive and often surprises travelers who didn’t measure their bags beforehand.

### Impact On Carry-on Eligibility

Your suitcase size also affects whether it can qualify as a carry-on. Carry-on limits are much smaller, generally around 22 x 14 x 9 inches, or about 45 linear inches. So, a 28 inch suitcase will almost always be too large to bring onboard as carry-on luggage.

Have you ever tried lifting a heavy suitcase into the overhead bin only to be stopped by gate agents? That’s why knowing the size rules ahead of time matters. If you want to avoid checking your bag, consider a smaller suitcase designed to fit under the seat or in the overhead compartment.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Are Linear Inches In Suitcase Measurements?

Linear inches measure the sum of a suitcase’s length, width, and height. Airlines use this to set size limits for checked and carry-on bags. It helps ensure your luggage fits their size policies.

### Does A 28 Inch Suitcase Equal 62 Linear Inches?

No, a 28 inch suitcase typically exceeds 62 linear inches. The 28 inches usually refer to height, not total linear inches. The combined length, width, and height often add up to more than 62 inches.

### Why Is 62 Linear Inches Important For Luggage?

Most airlines set 62 linear inches as the maximum size for checked bags. Bags larger than this may incur extra fees or be rejected. It’s a standard limit for avoiding oversized baggage charges.

### Can A 28 Inch Suitcase Fit Airline Size Restrictions?

It depends on the suitcase’s width and depth. A 28 inch height alone often exceeds 62 linear inches. Check the total dimensions to confirm if it meets airline rules.
