## Southwest Baggage Policy

Southwest Airlines has clear rules about baggage. Knowing these rules helps avoid surprises at the airport. Passengers get to bring bags without extra costs under certain limits. These rules include what counts as a checked bag and what can be carried on. Understanding Southwest's baggage policy makes travel smoother and stress-free.

### Standard Checked Bag Allowance

Southwest allows two free checked bags per passenger. Each bag must weigh 50 pounds or less. The maximum size is 62 inches total (length + width + height). Bags over this limit may face extra charges. A car seat does not count as one of these checked bags. It can be checked separately without fee.

### Carry-on Rules

Passengers may bring one carry-on bag and one personal item. The carry-on bag must fit in the overhead bin. The personal item fits under the seat in front. Items like purses, laptops, or small backpacks count as personal items. Car seats can be carried on if there is space. Otherwise, they should be checked at the gate.

### Additional Baggage Fees

Extra bags beyond the two free checked bags have fees. Each additional checked bag costs a set fee per flight. Oversized or overweight bags may have higher fees. Special items like sports gear or musical instruments might need extra charges. Always check fees before packing to avoid surprises.




## Car Seat Rules On Southwest

Southwest Airlines has clear rules for traveling with car seats. Knowing these rules helps you avoid extra fees and hassle. Car seats are treated differently depending on how you carry or check them.

Here is a simple guide to Southwest’s car seat rules. It covers checking, carrying on, and gate checking options.

### Car Seat As Checked Baggage

Southwest allows you to check a car seat without extra charges. It counts as one checked bag if you check it at the ticket counter. The car seat must be packed in a protective bag or box. This prevents damage during handling.

Checking your car seat can save space in the cabin. It also frees up your hands during boarding and flight.

### Car Seat As Carry-on

You can bring a car seat as a carry-on item. It must fit in the overhead bin or under the seat. Southwest permits a car seat in addition to your standard carry-on allowance. This option works well if you want to use the seat on board.

Make sure the car seat is FAA-approved for airplane use. This ensures it is safe and allowed during the flight.

### Gate Check Options

Gate checking a car seat is an option for Southwest travelers. You carry the car seat to the gate and give it to the staff before boarding. They tag and store it in the cargo hold. You pick it up at the gate after landing.

Gate check is convenient if the car seat is bulky or if overhead space is limited. It also reduces the risk of damage compared to regular checked baggage.

Southwest allows passengers to check one car seat free of charge. This policy applies when the car seat is checked separately from your regular bags. The car seat does not count as one of your checked bags.

Extra charges may occur if you check more than the allowed number of bags. Southwest permits two free checked bags per passenger. A car seat checked as an extra bag beyond this limit may incur fees.

### Damage Claims Process
