## American Airlines Baggage Policy

Understanding the American Airlines baggage policy is key to avoiding surprises at the airport. Knowing exactly what you can bring onboard helps you pack smart and travel stress-free. Let’s break down what’s allowed, especially if you want to carry both a backpack and a carry-on.

### Carry-on Allowance On American Airlines

American Airlines allows one carry-on bag and one personal item per passenger. The carry-on must fit in the overhead bin, with maximum dimensions of 22 x 14 x 9 inches. This size limit ensures your bag fits comfortably without causing delays.

Your personal item can be a backpack, purse, or laptop bag. It should fit under the seat in front of you, usually around 18 x 14 x 8 inches. Many travelers use a small backpack as their personal item, which means you can still bring a larger carry-on bag separately.

### Can You Bring Both A Backpack And A Carry-on?

Yes, you can bring both a backpack and a carry-on on American Airlines flights. The key is to consider which one counts as your personal item and which one is your carry-on. For example, if your backpack is small enough to fit under the seat, it counts as your personal item, allowing you to also bring a larger suitcase or carry-on.

Think about your packing style—do you prefer having essentials close by in your backpack? Many travelers I’ve met pack a backpack with electronics and travel documents, then check the carry-on bag, saving space and hassle. However, if both your backpack and carry-on are large, you might need to check one or pay extra fees.

### Fees And Restrictions To Watch For

American Airlines does not charge for your carry-on and personal item on most domestic flights. But, if your bag exceeds the size or weight limits, additional fees may apply. Checked bags always come with fees unless you have elite status or certain fare types.

Also, some international flights have different rules, so double-check before your trip. Are you flying with a lot of gear? Consider if it’s cheaper to check a bag or pay overweight fees. Planning ahead saves both money and headaches.
