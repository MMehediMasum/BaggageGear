## Alaska Airlines Baggage Policy

Understanding Alaska Airlines’ baggage policy can save you time and money during your travels. Knowing what counts as carry-on, checked baggage, and the fees involved helps you pack smartly and avoid surprises at the airport. Let’s break down the essentials of their luggage rules so you can travel with confidence.

### Carry-on Baggage Allowance

Alaska Airlines allows each passenger to bring one carry-on bag and one personal item on board for free. The carry-on bag must fit in the overhead bin and measure no more than 22 x 14 x 9 inches.

Your personal item, like a backpack or purse, should fit under the seat in front of you. Many travelers find this policy generous, especially for short trips where packing light is key.

Unlike carry-on bags, checked luggage usually comes with fees. The first checked bag typically costs $30, and the second bag is $40. Fees increase for additional bags or overweight luggage.

If you travel often, consider Alaska’s loyalty programs or credit cards, which can waive these fees. Have you ever been surprised by baggage fees at the gate? Planning ahead can avoid that hassle.

### Weight And Size Limits

Each checked bag must weigh under 50 pounds and measure less than 62 linear inches (length + width + height). Bags exceeding these limits face extra charges or may need repacking.

Checking your bag’s weight before heading to the airport can prevent unexpected fees. It’s one small step that can make a big difference in your travel budget.

### Special Items And Exceptions

Alaska Airlines allows some items like strollers, car seats, and medical equipment to be checked for free. Sports gear and musical instruments may have special handling rules and fees.

Knowing these exceptions ahead of time helps you prepare. Have special items on your trip? Double-checking Alaska’s guidelines will keep your journey smooth and stress-free.

### Overweight Baggage Fees

If your bag weighs more than the allowed limit, Alaska Airlines will charge an overweight baggage fee. The standard weight limit for checked bags is 50 pounds (23 kg). Bags weighing between 51 and 100 pounds (24 to 45 kg) usually incur a fee that can range from $100 to $200.

Think about your last trip—did you ever weigh your luggage before heading to the airport? It’s a simple step that can save you a lot of money. If you expect your bag to be heavy, consider splitting items into two bags or shipping some items separately.

### Oversized Item Charges

Bags that exceed the maximum dimensions—typically 62 linear inches (length + width + height)—are considered oversized. Alaska Airlines charges an additional fee for these bags, which can be as high as $200 per item. This includes large suitcases, sports equipment, and musical instruments.

Do you have bulky items like ski gear or a surfboard? Check Alaska Airlines’ specific guidelines because some oversized items may require special handling or advance notice. Planning ahead can keep your travel hassle-free and your wallet intact.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does Alaska Airlines Charge For Checked Bags?

Yes, Alaska Airlines charges for checked bags on most economy fares. The first checked bag fee is typically $30, and the second bag costs $40. Fees vary by route and fare type, so always check your specific ticket details before travel.

### Are Carry-on Bags Free On Alaska Airlines?

Yes, carry-on bags are free on Alaska Airlines. Each passenger can bring one carry-on bag and one personal item without extra charges. Ensure your carry-on meets size requirements to avoid fees at the gate.

### How Much Does Alaska Airlines Charge For Overweight Luggage?

Alaska Airlines charges overweight luggage fees if bags exceed 50 pounds. Fees start at $100 for bags weighing 51-100 pounds. Bags over 100 pounds may incur higher fees or may not be accepted.

### Can Alaska Airlines Baggage Fees Be Waived?

Baggage fees can be waived for elite Mileage Plan members and certain credit card holders. Military personnel also receive free checked bags. Check Alaska Airlines’ policies for eligibility and details on fee waivers.
