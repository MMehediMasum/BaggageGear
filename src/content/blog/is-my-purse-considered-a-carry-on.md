## Carry On Basics

Understanding the basics of carry on luggage helps avoid travel hassles. Airlines have rules about the size and type of items allowed on board. Knowing these rules makes packing easier and smoother at the airport. Your purse may or may not count as a carry on, based on these basics.

### Standard Carry On Dimensions

Most airlines set specific size limits for carry on bags. The usual maximum size is around 22 inches tall, 14 inches wide, and 9 inches deep. These dimensions fit most overhead bins on planes. Bags larger than this may need to be checked in. Small personal items, like purses, often have different size rules.

### Common Airline Policies

Many airlines allow one carry on bag plus one personal item. A purse usually counts as a personal item, not a carry on bag. Personal items must fit under the seat in front of you. Common examples include purses, small backpacks, or laptop bags. Airlines differ, so check their rules before packing. Overweight or oversized items can lead to extra fees.

Let’s break down what typical purse sizes look like and when your purse might be counted as a carry-on.

### Typical Purse Dimensions

Most purses fall within a size range that airlines consider a personal item rather than a full carry-on bag. Usually, purses measure around 10 to 12 inches in width, 7 to 9 inches in height, and 3 to 5 inches in depth.

Here’s a quick look at common purse dimensions:

| Dimension | Typical Range |
| --- | --- |
| Width | 10 - 12 inches (25 - 30 cm) |
| Height | 7 - 9 inches (18 - 23 cm) |
| Depth | 3 - 5 inches (7.5 - 13 cm) |

Have you ever tried stuffing a large tote or backpack in the overhead bin and struggled? That’s a sign your purse might not be just a personal item anymore.

### When A Purse Counts As Carry On

Your purse usually counts as a personal item, separate from your carry-on bag. But if it’s oversized, some airlines might classify it as a carry-on, especially if it’s bulky or looks like a small suitcase.

Consider these points to know when your purse might be counted as carry-on:

* **Dimensions exceed personal item limits:**Airlines often allow personal items up to 18 x 14 x 8 inches.
* **Multiple items:**Carrying both a large purse and a carry-on bag might push the purse into carry-on territory.
* **Airline rules:**Each airline has its own policies, so always check before you fly.

Think about your last flight—did the gate agent ask you to check your purse or place it with your carry-on? That experience can clue you in on how your purse is classified.

## Airline Rules On Personal Items

Understanding airline rules on personal items is important before packing. Airlines have specific guidelines about what counts as a carry-on and what counts as a personal item. Knowing these rules helps avoid extra fees and delays at the gate. Most airlines allow one carry-on bag plus one personal item per passenger. The size and type of these items matter. Personal items are usually smaller and meant to fit under the seat in front of you.

### Difference Between Carry On And Personal Item

A carry-on bag is larger and goes in the overhead bin. It can hold clothes, shoes, and other travel essentials. A personal item is smaller, like a purse, backpack, or laptop bag. It must fit under the seat in front of you. Airlines often measure personal items to make sure they fit. Personal items should hold essentials like your wallet, phone, and travel documents.

### Popular Airline Regulations

* **American Airlines:**Carry-on size limit is 22 x 14 x 9 inches; personal items must fit under the seat.
* **Delta Airlines:**Carry-on size up to 22 x 14 x 9 inches; personal items include purses and briefcases.
* **United Airlines:**Carry-on bags can be 22 x 14 x 9 inches; personal items must be smaller.
* **Southwest Airlines:**Allows one carry-on plus one personal item such as a purse or laptop bag.
* **JetBlue:**Carry-on max size is 22 x 14 x 9 inches; personal items must fit under the seat.




## Packing Tips For Your Purse

![](https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=packing-your-purse-for-travel-can-be-tricky.-you-want-to-have-everything-you-need-without-overloading-yourself.&w=424&h=324&c=7)

Packing your purse for travel can be tricky. You want to have everything you need without overloading yourself. Smart packing makes your purse both functional and easy to carry through the airport.

### Essentials To Carry

Start by including your travel documents such as your passport, boarding pass, and ID. Add a small wallet or cardholder to keep cash and cards organized.

Don’t forget your phone, charger, and headphones for entertainment and communication. A small pack of tissues and hand sanitizer can be lifesavers in busy airports.

Think about comfort items like lip balm or a travel-size lotion. These small touches can make a big difference during long trips.

### Maximizing Space Efficiently

Use small pouches or zippered compartments to separate items inside your purse. This helps you avoid digging around when you need something quickly.

Roll or fold items like scarves or light clothing to save space. Avoid bulky items unless absolutely necessary.

Ask yourself which items you really need at hand. Could heavier or less-used things fit better in your main carry-on bag?

## Avoiding Extra Fees

Extra fees can quickly add up during travel. Airlines charge for bags that exceed size or weight limits. Knowing if your purse counts as a carry-on helps avoid surprises. Staying within the allowed limits keeps your travel budget safe. Here are ways to manage your bags and avoid extra charges.

### How To Stay Within Limits

* Check airline rules for carry-on and personal item sizes.

* Use a lightweight purse or bag to reduce overall weight.
* Keep essential items only in your purse to avoid overpacking.

* Pack only what you need for the trip duration.
* Use packing cubes to organize and limit your items.
* Avoid carrying duplicates or unnecessary gadgets in your purse.

* Review your purse contents before leaving to remove extras.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is A Purse Allowed As A Carry-on Item?

Yes, most airlines allow a purse as a personal item or carry-on. It must fit under the seat or in overhead bins.

### Can I Bring A Purse And A Carry-on Bag?

Usually, airlines permit one carry-on bag plus one personal item like a purse. Check your airline’s specific rules before traveling.

### What Size Purse Qualifies As A Carry-on?

A purse should be small enough to fit under the seat in front of you. Dimensions vary by airline but typically under 18x14x8 inches.

### Do Airlines Charge Extra For Carrying A Purse?

No, airlines generally do not charge extra for a purse if it qualifies as a personal item. Fees may apply for additional carry-ons.
